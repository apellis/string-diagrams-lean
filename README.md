# string-diagrams-lean

A Lean 4 library for diagrammatic reasoning: typed diagram syntax, presentations by generators and relations, interpretations, and Lean-checked diagram rewriting.

## Contents

### Core

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `StringDiagrams.Signature` | `Signature`: regions (0-cells), strand colours (1-cells), generators (2-cells) with typed boundaries and a parity; objects `Obj` and layers `Layer` (`id ⊗ g ⊗ id`) with well-formedness. Monoidal signatures are the case of a single region. |
| `StringDiagrams.Syntax` | The free 2-category: morphisms are well-typed lists of layers (`Diagram`), composed by concatenation; whiskering and retyping along equalities of objects (which never changes the underlying layers, so widths may be symbolic).; boundaries of a diagram are determined by its layers (`Diagram.eq_dom_of_layers_eq_cons`, `Diagram.eq_cod_of_layers_eq_append`). |
| `StringDiagrams.Presentation` | `Presentation S R`: relations between `R`-linear combinations of diagrams. `Presentation.Presented` is the `R`-linear category obtained by quotienting by the tensor ideal generated by the relations and the (Koszul-signed) interchange law. `lin_rel_cast`, `diag_interchange`: relations and interchange at arbitrary whiskered positions. |
| `StringDiagrams.Interpretation` | Functors determined by images of layers; `Presentation.lift` (soundness): a functor killing all whiskered relations and interchange instances descends to an `R`-linear functor on the presented category. The linear extension `freeLift` on whiskered combinations (`freeLift_map_whisker_of`, `freeLift_map_whisker_sub_smul`). |
| `StringDiagrams.Whisker` | Whiskering `u ⊗ f ⊗ v` on the presented category (`Presentation.whisk`), linear and functorial; whiskering twice is whiskering once by the composite objects (`whisk_whisk`). |
| `StringDiagrams.Interchange` | The interchange law for arbitrary diagrams in monoidal signatures: `(f ⊗ 1) ≫ (1 ⊗ g) = (-1)^{|f||g|} (1 ⊗ g) ≫ (f ⊗ 1)` (`Presentation.diag_interchange_diagrams`), derived from the single-generator instances. |
| `StringDiagrams.Horizontal` | The interchange law for arbitrary diagrams and general regions (2-categories): for `f : a ⟶ a'`, `g : b ⟶ b'` with `a` ending in the region where `b` starts (`Obj.Composable`), `(f ⊗ 1_b) ≫ (1_{a'} ⊗ g) = (-1)^{|f||g|} (1_a ⊗ g) ≫ (f ⊗ 1_{b'})` (`Presentation.diag_interchange_of_composable`), with the whiskered diagrams `Diagram.rwhisker`, `Diagram.lwhisker`. |
| `StringDiagrams.Bicategory` | Horizontal structure of presented 2-categories with general regions: right/left whiskering `Presentation.wRAt`, `Presentation.wL` and horizontal composition `Presentation.hcomp` on the presented category, with functoriality, strict associativity and unit laws, and the (Koszul-signed) interchange law. For even signatures, `P.Bicat` is a strict bicategory (Mathlib `Bicategory`, `Bicategory.Strict`) whose objects are regions, 1-morphisms well-formed words (`Presentation.Bicat.Hom`), and 2-morphisms the morphisms of `P.Presented`. |
| `StringDiagrams.Grading` | Gradings `deg : S.Gen → A`: degrees of diagrams; for homogeneous presentations the tensor ideal is homogeneous (`homogeneousComponent_mem_ideal`) and Hom-spaces decompose as internal direct sums `P.homDeg deg a b d` (`isInternal_homDeg`); endomorphism algebras are graded algebras (`gradedAlgebra`). Examples: free commuting dots (dot 2; `FreeDots.gradedAlgebra`), Temperley–Lieb (cup `t`, cap `−t`). |
| `StringDiagrams.Monoidal` | For even monoidal signatures (`Signature.IsEven`), `P.Presented` is a strict monoidal `R`-linear category (Mathlib `MonoidalCategory`, `MonoidalLinear`): `P.obj a ⊗ P.obj b = P.obj (a.tensor b)`, associators and unitors are `eqToIso`, `diag_whiskerRight`/`diag_whiskerLeft`. Examples: free commuting dots (`x R 1 0 ▷ FD.obj R 1 = x R 2 0`; commutativity of neighbouring dots from `whisker_exchange`, `x_mul_x_comm_two`), Temperley–Lieb (one strand is self-dual: `ExactPairing` from cup and cap, `η ≫ ε = δ`). |
| `StringDiagrams.Derivation` | Local super-derivations (diagrammatically local differentials, as in Ellis–Qi, arXiv:1504.01712v2, §§2.2–3.1): values `δ g` on generators of parity `|g| + 1` extend by the super Leibniz rule (`derivFree`); for monoidal presentations with homogeneous relations killed by `d`, the derivation descends to the presented category (`LocalDerivation.derivFree_mem_ideal`, `Presentation.deriv`), satisfies `d(f g) = d(f) g + (-1)^{|f|} f d(g)` (`deriv_mul`), commutes with whiskering, and `d² = 0` follows from its vanishing on generators (`deriv_deriv_eq_zero`). Examples: odd dots with `d(x) = x²`; the exterior example with `d(x) = 1`. |
| `StringDiagrams.Generation` | Induction principles: morphisms are linear combinations of diagrams, diagrams are composites of layers (`Presentation.hom_induction_layers`); linear maps on Hom modules are determined by classes of diagrams (`linearMap_ext_diag`). For a set `C` of objects closed under layers (e.g. a fixed number of strands when generators preserve it), morphisms from `C` to its complement vanish (`eq_zero_of_mem_of_not_mem`, `eq_zero_of_invariant`) and morphisms within `C` lie in every composition-closed family of submodules containing identities and single layers (`mem_of_layers`). |
| `StringDiagrams.Transport` | Retyping along equalities of objects: `Presentation.castHom` (compatible with composition, whiskering and classes of diagrams; an `R`-linear equivalence `castLinearEquiv`, an algebra isomorphism `endCast` on endomorphisms), `lin_cast`; diagrams with the same layers have the same class up to retyping (`diag_eq_castHom_of_layers_eq`, `diag_transfer`). If every layer out of `a` ends at `a`, `End (P.obj a)` is generated by single layers as an algebra (`mem_subalgebra_of_layers`). |
| `StringDiagrams.Positional` | Relations for diagrams given by their lists of layers: a layer is determined by its boundary, left width and generator (`Layer.eq_of_dom_eq`); the interchange law at an arbitrary position with the Koszul sign, for general regions (`Presentation.diag_interchange_of_layers`, `_of_even`, `_of_odd`); the quotient map and whiskering as algebra homomorphisms of endomorphism algebras (`linAlgHom`, `whiskAlgHom`, `LinDiagram.whiskerAlgHom`). |
| `StringDiagrams.InterchangeLayers` | The interchange law for two diagrams given by their lists of layers, side by side at an arbitrary position (`u ⊗ F ⊗ m ⊗ G ⊗ v`), for general regions, when the generators facing each other are even (`Presentation.diag_interchange_layers_of_even`); replacing a block of layers by one with the same image (`Presentation.diag_eq_of_layers_eq_append`). |
| `StringDiagrams.SingleColour` | Signatures with one region and one colour: objects `strands n` and layers `lay i g j` (`dlay`: its diagram between numbers of strands) in normal form, with equalities of objects and layers reduced to equalities of widths (`obj_eq_iff`, `layer_eq_iff`, `whisker_strands`, `lay_whisker`); defining relations and the interchange law at symbolic positions (`relation_at`, `interchange_at`, `interchange_at_of_even`, `interchange_at_of_odd`); for width-preserving generators, vanishing of morphisms between different widths (`eq_zero_of_length_ne`) and generation of `End (strands n)` by single layers (`mem_subalgebra_of_dlay`). |
| `StringDiagrams.EckmannHilton` | Endomorphisms of an identity 1-morphism (empty word `Obj.nil r`, the unit object in the monoidal case) commute for even signatures (`Presentation.nil_comp_comm`, `nilEndCommRing`, `unit_comp_comm`) and supercommute in general (`nil_comp_comm_super`: `f ≫ g = (-1)^{pq} g ≫ f` for parities `p`, `q`). |
| `StringDiagrams.BicatHom` | The 2-morphisms of `P.Bicat` are the morphisms of `P.Presented`: `rfl` lemmas (`Presentation.Bicat.comp_def`, `id_def`, `add_def`, `sub_def`, `neg_def`, `zero_def`, `smul_def`, and `toHom_*`) for rewriting between the two hom types. |
| `StringDiagrams.LocalInterpretation` | Representations by local operators (`LocalInterpretation`): objects go to modules `M (κ a)` indexed by a function of the object, layers act by local operators on an ambient module. The image of any diagram is the composite of its layer operators (`functor_map_hom`, and `functor_map_transport` along arbitrary equalities of indices); soundness reduces to the vanishing of a linear evaluation of each whiskered relation (`respects_of`, `evalW`), and the interchange part to commutation of generators at disjoint positions with the Koszul sign (`evalW_interchange_eq_zero`). Special cases: one module for all objects (`uniform`), index-preserving componentwise operators on `Π i, M i` (`pi`, `pi_functor_map_transport`). Pointwise evaluation without composites of linear maps (`opList_cons_apply`, `evalW_of_apply`, `uniform_evalW_of_apply`; `opList_singleton` leaves no trailing identity); linear extensions of functors to modules at a vector (`freeLift_map_hom_apply`, `freeLift_map_whisker_sub_smul_hom_apply`). Over `ℤ`: `Presentation.int_smul_eq_zsmul`, `Presentation.hom_induction_int`. Used by the polynomial representation of free commuting dots (`uniform`) and the Temperley–Lieb representation. |
| `StringDiagrams.IndexedInterpretation` | Interpretations in an arbitrary category with objects `X (κ a)` indexed by an invariant `κ` preserved by every layer (e.g. the number of strands), layers acting by endomorphisms `A L i`. Transport lemma: for any interpretation with `I.obj a = X (κ a)` whose layers are, after transport, the `A L (κ L.dom)` (`Interpretation.LayerTransport`), the image of a diagram transported to `X i` is the product `endList A i (layers f)` of the endomorphisms of its layers (`Interpretation.map_transport`); the canonical such interpretation `Interpretation.ofEnd`. Linear evaluation `linEnd`, `linEndW` in `End (X i)` (`Interpretation.freeLift_map_transport`); soundness reduced to it (`Interpretation.respects_of_linEndW`, with `linEndW_interchange_eq_zero` from Koszul-signed commutation); the descended functor on diagrams (`Interpretation.lift_diag_transport`). |
| `StringDiagrams.Linear.MatEnd` | Matrices of morphisms `MatEnd X = ⨁_{i,j} Hom(X i, X j)` of a finite family of objects in an `R`-linear category, an `R`-algebra with `(f * g) i l = ∑ j, g i j ≫ f j l` (the category algebra of the family); matrix units `single` (`single_mul_single`, `one_eq_sum_single`, `eq_sum_single`), the complete set of orthogonal idempotents `idem i` (`sum_idem`, `idem_mul_mul_idem`); functoriality `MatEnd.map` along linear functors; `MatEnd.biproductAlgEquiv : End (⨁ Y) ≃ₐ[R] MatEnd Y` (the endomorphism algebra of a finite direct sum, `π i ≫ ι i ↦ idem i`); block-diagonal assembly `MatEnd.blockMap` of algebra homomorphisms along functorial transports. |
| `StringDiagrams.Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Linear.Opposite` | The opposite of an `R`-linear category is `R`-linear (`CategoryTheory.Linear.opposite`, `op_smul`, `unop_smul` definitional), with `R`-linear `Functor.op`, `leftOp`, `rightOp`, `unop`. |
| `StringDiagrams.Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Linear.End` | `Functor.mapEndAlgHom : End X →ₐ[R] End (F.obj X)` for a linear functor, `Iso.conjAlgEquiv : End X ≃ₐ[R] End Y`, `ModuleCat.endAlgEquiv : End M ≃ₐ[R] Module.End R M`; for a linear functor to modules the representations `Functor.mapModuleEnd : End X →ₐ[R] Module.End R (F.obj X)`, `Functor.mapModuleEndOfIso` (transported along `F.obj X ≅ M`, e.g. `eqToIso`) and `Functor.mapModuleHom : (X ⟶ Y) →ₗ[R] (F.obj X →ₗ[R] F.obj Y)`. |

### Functors from layer maps

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `StringDiagrams.LayerMap.Basic` | Soundness from unwhiskered relations: for a family of functors compatible with whiskering, `Presentation.Respects` follows from the vanishing of the relations and of the interchange law without whiskering (`Presentation.respects_of_whisker`, `Presentation.respects_of_whisker_on`). Layer maps `LayerMap S S'` (images of objects and of layers; the image of a layer may depend on its position), the induced functors of free 2-categories (`LayerMap.functor`) and, with weights `χ : S.Gen → R` of the generators, to presented categories (`LayerMap.toPresented`: `d ↦ weight χ d • [φ d]`); compatibility with whiskering (`LayerMap.WhiskerData`, for families of layer maps and on a class of admissible objects), the induced functors `P.Presented ⥤ Q.Presented` (`LayerMap.lift`, `lift_diag`, `lift_whisk`); the interchange law from images of instances of it (`toPresented_interchange`, `toPresented_interchange_swap`); degree and parity preservation (`LayerMap.PreservesDeg`, `lift_mem_homDeg`, `preservesDeg_parityDeg`). |
| `StringDiagrams.LayerMap.Op` | Contravariant layer maps (`OpLayerMap`): reflections in a horizontal axis, reversing the order of the layers of a diagram; functors `Obj S ⥤ (Obj S')ᵒᵖ` and `P.Presented ⥤ Q.Presentedᵒᵖ` (`OpLayerMap.lift`), with compatibility with whiskering, the interchange law for reflections and for rotations by a half turn (`toPresented_interchange_reflect`, `toPresented_interchange_rotate`), and degrees. The opposite of an `R`-linear category is `R`-linear. |
| `StringDiagrams.LayerMap.Generators` | Layer maps induced by maps of regions, strands and generators: covariant (`SigMap`), reflections in a horizontal axis (`SigFlip`, `g : w ⟶ w'` goes to `κ w' ⟶ κ w`) and in a vertical axis (`SigMirror`, reversing the order of 1-morphisms: words are relabelled and read backwards, regions on the left and right are exchanged); endomorphisms of a signature fixing regions and strands leave objects unchanged (`SigMap.ofGen`, `SigMap.id`, `SigFlip.ofGen`). Each is compatible with whiskering and, if it preserves parities, respects the interchange law (also for odd generators), so it induces a functor as soon as the relations of `P` are sent to zero (`SigMap.lift`, `SigFlip.lift`, `SigMirror.lift`); the mirror reverses whiskering (`SigMirror.lift_whisk`). Koszul signs for horizontal composites `f ⊗ g = (f ⊗ 1) ≫ (1 ⊗ g)`: a map of signatures commutes with them (`SigMap.map_tensor`), while `σ (f ⊗ g) = (-1)^{\|f\|\|g\|} σ g ⊗ σ f` for the mirror (`SigMirror.diag_map_tensor`) and `ψ (f ⊗ g) = (-1)^{\|f\|\|g\|} ψ f ⊗ ψ g` for the flip (`SigFlip.diag_map_tensor`). |
| `StringDiagrams.LayerMap.Local` | Local maps (`LocalMap`): images of layers that are arbitrary morphisms of the target presented category, extended to diagrams by composition (`LocalMap.functor`); compatibility with whiskering on layers extends to diagrams (`LocalMap.WhiskerData.functor_map_whisker`), giving functors on presented categories (`LocalMap.lift`, `lift_layer`, `lift_whisk`). From images of generators (`LocalMap.ofGen`: `u ⊗ g ⊗ v ↦ κ u ⊗ img g ⊗ κ v`): automatic compatibility with whiskering, and the interchange law when every `img g` is homogeneous of the parity of `g` (`LocalMap.ofGen_interchange`, by the super interchange law; automatic for even signatures, `LocalMap.parity_of_isEven`); the resulting functor `LocalMap.liftGen` only needs the relations of `P` to be sent to zero. Degree (and parity) preservation: `LocalMap.PreservesDeg`, `ofGen_preservesDeg`, `lift_mem_homDeg`. |

### Monoidal supercategories and 2-supercategories

Following J. Brundan, A. P. Ellis, *Monoidal supercategories* ([arXiv:1603.05928v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05928)). Mathlib's `MonoidalCategory` and `Bicategory` contain the ordinary interchange law, so the super versions are separate `Prop` classes over Mathlib's `MonoidalCategoryStruct` and a data class `BicategoryStruct`.

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `StringDiagrams.Super.Functor` | Definition 1.1(ii)–(iii), (v): superfunctors (`Supercategory.IsSuperfunctor`: linear, parity-preserving), supernatural transformations given by their homogeneous components with the sign rule `F f ≫ x_{p} = (-1)^{p|f|} x_{p} ≫ G f` (`Supercategory.SuperNatTrans`), vertical composition, even supernatural transformations as natural transformations with even components; the Koszul sign `koszulSign p q`. |
| `StringDiagrams.Super.Monoidal` | Definition 1.4: `MonoidalSupercategory R C` (Mathlib's monoidal axioms except `tensor_comp`, functoriality of whiskering, the super interchange law `f ▷ Y ≫ X' ◁ g = (-1)^{|f||g|} (X ◁ g ≫ f ▷ Y')`, linear and even whiskering, even coherence maps), `IsStrict`; the super interchange law (1.1) for the paper's tensor product `superTensorHom f g = X ◁ g ≫ f ▷ Y'` (`superTensorHom_comp_superTensorHom`) and for Mathlib's `tensorHom`; without odd morphisms, monoidal supercategories are Mathlib monoidal linear categories and conversely (`toMonoidalCategory`, `ofMonoidalCategory`); monoidal superfunctors and monoidal natural transformations (Definition 1.4(ii)–(iii)). |
| `StringDiagrams.Super.MonoidalEquivalence` | The paragraph after Definition 1.4: composition of monoidal superfunctors (`MonoidalSuperfunctor.comp`), vertical composition of monoidal natural transformations (`MonoidalNatTrans.vcomp`), monoidal natural isomorphisms (`MonoidalNatIso`; the inverse of an invertible monoidal natural transformation is monoidal: `MonoidalNatIso.ofNatIso`), monoidal superequivalences (`MonoidalSuperequivalence`: monoidal superfunctors `F`, `G` with `G ∘ F`, `F ∘ G` isomorphic to the identities via monoidal natural transformations), whose underlying superfunctor is a superequivalence (`MonoidalSuperequivalence.superequivalence`). |
| `StringDiagrams.Super.MonoidalSuperequivalenceInverse` | The paragraph after Definition 1.4 ("equivalently, there is a monoidal superfunctor `F : A → B` which defines a superequivalence"): for a monoidal superfunctor `F` and a superequivalence `(G, η, ε)` on it (`Supercategory.Superequivalence`), `F` is fully faithful (`Superequivalence.fullyFaithful`) and the quasi-inverse `G` is a monoidal superfunctor (`Superequivalence.inverseMonoidal`), with `c_G`, `i_G` determined by `F(c_G) = ε⁻¹ ∘ (ε ⊗ ε) ∘ c_F⁻¹`, `F(i_G) = ε⁻¹ ∘ i_F⁻¹` (`Superequivalence.map_inverseMonoidal_μIso_hom`, `Superequivalence.map_inverseMonoidal_εIso_hom`); the counit is monoidal (`Superequivalence.counitMonoidalNatTrans`), and so is the unit adjusted to satisfy the triangle identity with `ε` (`Superequivalence.adjointUnitIso`, `Superequivalence.unitMonoidalNatTrans`; it is `η` when `(η, ε)` already satisfies the triangle identity, `Superequivalence.adjointUnitIso_eq`). Hence `F` underlies a monoidal superequivalence (`MonoidalSuperequivalence.ofSuperequivalence`), and the two formulations of monoidal superequivalence agree (`MonoidalSuperequivalence.nonempty_iff_exists_superequivalence`). Helpers: the interchange law without sign for an even and an arbitrary morphism (`MonoidalSupercategory.interchange_even_left`, `MonoidalSupercategory.interchange_even_right`); images of whiskerings and coherence maps under a monoidal superfunctor (`MonoidalSuperfunctor.map_whiskerLeft`, `map_whiskerRight`, `map_associator_hom`, `map_leftUnitor_hom`, `map_rightUnitor_hom`). |

**Erratum (Definition 2.2(iii); also Theorem 5.5, Remarks 4.10 and 5.7, and the §6 analogue of Theorem 5.5).** Printed: 2-supercategories, 2-superfunctors and 2-natural transformations form a 2-category `2-𝔖ℭ𝔄𝔗`, where 2-natural transformations are oplax (footnote to Definition 2.2(iii)); Theorem 5.5 speaks of the strict 2-categories `Π-2-ℭ𝔄𝔗` and `Π-2-𝔖ℭ𝔄𝔗`, and Remarks 4.10 and 5.7 of strict 3-(super)categories one level higher. Corrected statement: these are sesquicategories (1-truncations, categories of 2-morphisms, and whiskerings on both sides; with supermodifications, Gray-categories). Theorem 5.5 and its §6 analogue are formalized accordingly: `𝔻₂`, `𝔼₂` are mutually inverse equivalences of the 1-truncations (Lemma 5.4) which are bijective on 2-morphisms, compatibly with identities and vertical composition (`PiTwoCat.toPiTwoSCat_isTwoEquivalence`, `QPiTwoCat.toQPiTwoGSCat_isTwoEquivalence`). The vertical composite of 2-natural transformations is moreover associative and unital only up to the associators and unitors of the target unless the target is strict.

**Erratum (Remark 5.7, last sentence).** Printed: the monoidal category underlying the Drinfeld center of a Π-2-supercategory `𝔄` is monoidally equivalent to the Drinfeld center of `𝔄̲`. The deduction from the 3-equivalence of Remark 5.7 identifies `Z(𝔄)̲` with the endomorphisms of the identity in the 3-category of Π-2-categories, that is, with the strong *Π-2-natural* transformations of the identity of `𝔄̲` (Definition 5.2(iii)) and their modifications, the Π-center of `𝔄̲`; with that target the statement holds (`DrinfeldCenter.centerEquivalence`; the comparison functor is an isomorphism of monoidal categories). **Errata (§6).**

1. *Corollary 6.7(iii).* Printed: `γ_G ∘ Q'x = xQ ∘ γ_G`. Corrected: `γ_G ∘ Q'x = xQ ∘ γ_F` (`QPiSupercategory.γ_naturality_supernatural`); the printed right-hand side is not a composable pair unless `F = G`.
2. *Proof of Corollary 6.7(i).* The adjunction composite is printed as `Q ii⁻¹ ∘ jj Q : Q ⇒ QQ⁻¹Q ⇒ Q`, and similarly for `Q⁻¹`; the composite `Q ⇒ QQ⁻¹Q ⇒ Q` is `jj Q ∘ Q ii⁻¹` (order of composition). The statement is unaffected (`QPiSupercategory.Q_map_ii`, `QPiSupercategory.ii_Qinv`, `QPiSupercategory.qAdjunction`).
3. *Proof of Theorem 6.13, the associated graded `(Q, Π)`-supercategory.* With the degree conventions of Definition 6.4 (`σ : Q ⇒ I` of degree `-1`), the morphisms `λ → μ` of degree `m` and parity `a` must be `Hom_A(λ, Q^{-m} Π^a μ)`, not `Hom_A(λ, Q^m Π^a μ)` as printed: the isomorphism `σ_λ : Qλ → λ` comes from `1_{Qλ}` and must have degree `-1`. `QAssociated` uses the corrected convention. Its Π-part is the associated Π-supercategory of §5, with the corrected sign of the §5 erratum (the composite of two odd morphisms carries `-ξ`).
4. *Definition 6.12(ii) and Theorem 6.13.* As printed, a `(Q, Π)`-functor is a Π-functor `(F, β_F)` with a natural isomorphism `γ_F : Q'F ≅ FQ` (`QPiFunctorData`). The construction of `𝔻` on 1-morphisms in the proof of Theorem 6.13 needs `γ_F` to be compatible with `β`: `γ_F Π ∘ Q'β_F ∘ β_{Q'} F = F β_Q ∘ β_F Q ∘ Π'γ_F`, i.e. `γ_F` is Π-natural (`QPiFunctorData.IsCompatible`). This holds for every `𝔼(F)` (`QPiSupercategory.β_γ_compat`) and is invariant under `(Q, Π)`-natural isomorphism (`QPiFunctorData.IsCompatible.of_iso`), but it does not follow from the printed axioms: `QPiCompat.twisted` satisfies them without being compatible (`QPiCompat.twisted_not_isCompatible`), so with the printed definition `𝔼` is not essentially surjective on 1-morphisms and Theorem 6.13 fails (`QPiCompat.twisted_not_iso_image`). 5. *Definition 6.10.* Printed: "the 2-morphisms `σ_λ`, `σ̄_λ` and `ξ_λ` are induced by `1_{1_λ}`". The third 2-morphism is `ζ_λ : π_λ ⇒ 1_λ` (as in Definition 4.4); `ξ_λ = ζ_λζ_λ` is `-1` (`TwoEnvelope.toHom_ξ`), not induced by `1_{1_λ}`. `QPiTwoEnvelope.instQPiTwoSupercategory` uses `ζ_λ`.
6. *Proof of Lemma 6.11.* The 2-morphism `ℝ̃(x^{n,b}_{m,a})` is printed as a 2-morphism `ℝ̃(Π^aF) ⇒ ℝ̃(Π^bG)`; its source and target are `ℝ̃(Q^mΠ^aF)` and `ℝ̃(Q^nΠ^bG)` (`QPiTwoEnvelope.extend_map₂`). The displayed formula `(σ^nζ^b)⁻¹(ℝG) ∘ ℝx ∘ σ^mζ^a(ℝF)` is correct.

**Definition (not in the paper): `(Q, Π)`-2-functors.** The paper leaves the §6 analogue of Theorem 5.5 to the reader and does not define the 1- and 2-morphisms between `(Q, Π)`-2-categories (Definition 6.14). We define a `(Q, Π)`-2-functor `𝔄 → 𝔅` (`QPiTwoFunctor`) as a Π-2-functor `(ℝ, j)` (Definition 5.2(ii)) together with 2-isomorphisms `k_λ : q_{ℝλ} ≅ ℝq_λ` such that `ℝγ_F ∘ c ∘ (ℝF)k_μ = c ∘ k_λ(ℝF) ∘ γ_{ℝF}` for every 1-morphism `F : λ → μ` (the analogue for `(q, γ)` of the first axiom of Definition 5.2(ii)), and a `(Q, Π)`-2-natural transformation `(X, x) : ℝ ⇒ 𝕊` (`QPiTwoFunctor.IsQPiTwoNatural`) as a Π-2-natural transformation (Definition 5.2(iii)) with `x_{q_λ} ∘ X_λ k ∘ γ_{X_λ} = k X_λ`. As for erratum 4, a bare isomorphism `q_{ℝλ} ≅ ℝq_λ` would not suffice: the axiom on `k` holds for `𝔼(ℝ)` for every graded 2-superfunctor `ℝ` (`TwoSuperfunctor.kγ_comm`) and is used for the naturality of the coherence maps of `𝔻(ℝ)` with respect to the 2-morphisms `σ`, so it is necessary for `𝔼 ∘ 𝔻 = I`; similarly the condition on `(X, x)` holds for `𝔼(X, x)` (`TwoNatTrans.isQPiTwoNatural`) and is needed for `𝔻(X, x)` to be supernatural. The 2-categorical form of the compatibility adopted in erratum 4 — `k` is Π-natural, i.e. compatible with `j` — is not imposed separately: it follows from the first axiom of Definition 5.2(ii) at `F = q_λ` and the naturality of `β` (`QPiTwoFunctor.j_comm`), and makes the functors `ℋom(λ, μ) → ℋom(ℝλ, ℝμ)` `(Q, Π)`-functors in the adopted sense (`QPiTwoFunctor.homQPi`). As in Definition 6.12(ii), no compatibility of `k` with `ii`, `jj` is required.

### Chord diagrams

Planar chord diagrams with all endpoints on one boundary, built from cups and crossings, and a
combinatorial normal form up to isotopy and braid/pitchfork moves (following the argument of
A. Lauda, arXiv:0803.3652v3, §8, and M. Khovanov, A. Lauda, arXiv:0807.3250v1, §3.2.3).

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |

| `StringDiagrams.Chord.NormalForm` | `Fits`, `run`, `canon_cup`, `canon_ilv`; the normal form theorem `equiv_canon_or_reducible`: every diagram is equivalent to `canon` of its final state or is reducible. |

### Example: chord diagrams with weights

`StringDiagrams.Examples.ChordDiagrams`: regions the integers (weights), letters `E`, `F` shifting weights by `±2`, a cup `1 ⟶ a (d a)` and a crossing `a b ⟶ b a` in every weight (`gens : RegionChordGens`). Imposing the braid and pitchfork relations in every weight (`pres`), every chord diagram equals, after retyping, a diagram with a double crossing, one with a curl, or the canonical diagram of its pairing (`near_canon_or_reducible`, from `RegionChordGens.near_canon_or_reducible` with the trivial filtration).

### Example: the walking biadjunction

`StringDiagrams.Examples.WalkingBiadjunction`: strands `E : a → b`, `F : b → a` with all four cups and caps and a dot on `E` whose two rotations agree; every 2-morphism is cyclic (`pivotal`), the dot slides along both cups, and the plain bubble equals the trace of `snakeDown ≫ snakeUp` (`bubble_eq_trace_snakes`), a consequence of trace cyclicity.

### Example: a cyclic generator with a two-letter boundary

`StringDiagrams.Examples.TwoStrandGenerator`: strands `E : a → b`, `F : b → a` with `E* = F` and a generator `s : E F ⟶ E F`, presented as the cyclic pivotal extension (the two rotations of `s` by the nested cups and caps of `E F` are identified); the presented bicategory is pivotal in one line (`pivotal`). Derived identities of explicit diagrams: the zigzag identity for the nested cups and caps of `E F` (`zigzagEF_eq_id`), and `s` slides along the nested cups of `E F` from either copy of `E F` to the other, becoming its rotation (`s_slide_cup`, `s_slide_cup'`, the latter using cyclicity).

### Example: odd dots

`StringDiagrams.Examples.Exterior`: a single odd dot with `dot ≫ dot = 0`; distinct dots anticommute by the super interchange law alone (`x_mul_x_anticomm`).

### Example: free commuting dots

`StringDiagrams.Examples.FreeDots`: a single even dot and no relations. Dots on distinct strands commute by the interchange law alone (`x_mul_x_comm`, for symbolic width and positions); the presented category is strict monoidal (`FD.obj_tensor`, `x_whiskerRight`, `whiskerLeft_x`, `x_whiskerRight_strands`).

### Example: bubbles in weighted regions

`StringDiagrams.Examples.WeightedBubbles`: a small test of the region-aware part of the library, loosely modelled on the `sl₂` case of Khovanov–Lauda's 2-category ([arXiv:0807.3250](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.3250)) but not a formalization of it. Regions are weights in `ℤ`, strands `E`, `F` change the weight by `2`, and a bubble in the region `λ` evaluates to `c λ` for a given `c : ℤ → R`. Two bubbles on either side of a strand drawn at interleaved heights evaluate to `c (μ + 4) c (μ + 2)` (`interleaved`, via the interchange law for general regions); in the presented bicategory, `bubble ▷ E` and `E ◁ bubble` are distinguished by a scalar representation whenever the two regions have different values (`leftBubble_ne_rightBubble`).

### Example: Temperley–Lieb

`StringDiagrams.Examples.TemperleyLieb`: one self-dual colour with a cup and a cap, the two zigzag relations and the loop relation `cap ∘ cup = δ`. ### Example: the odd Brauer supercategory

`StringDiagrams.Examples.OddBrauer`: Brundan–Ellis, Example 1.5(iii) (the marked Brauer category of Kujawa–Tharp): an even crossing and odd cup and cap with the relations of the paper (`s² = 1`, braid, zigzags `1` and `-1`, a strand sliding through a cup, `cup ≫ s = cup`), a strict monoidal supercategory (`OddBrauer.monoidalSupercategory`). Using the relations and the super interchange law: `s ≫ cap = -cap` (`cross_comp_cap`), hence the bubble is minus itself, `2 • bubble = 0` over any commutative ring (`two_smul_bubble`), and `bubble = 0` when `2` is invertible (`bubble_eq_zero`).

## Tactics

`StringDiagrams.Tactic.WordRw` (generic, for any ring): `word_rw [e₁, ← e₂]` rewrites with equations between words of generators modulo associativity, inside linear combinations, with side conditions discharged by `omega` (configurable); `word_comm [c₁, …]` decides equality of linear combinations of words modulo given (conditional) commutations, via normal forms in partially commutative monoids. Tests in `StringDiagrams.Tactic.Test` (generic rings, and the Temperley–Lieb, free-dots and odd-dots examples at symbolic positions).

## Notation and drawings

`StringDiagrams.DSL` provides a one-line notation for layered diagrams, e.g.
`[i i j] | x@0 ; psi@1 ; psi@0` (source object, then layers `generator@position` from
bottom to top), with a printer, a parser and the round-trip theorem `DSL.parse_print`.
`StringDiagrams.Render` draws diagrams as SVG (embedding the notation as metadata) and
TikZ. Drawings are presentation only; they are not evidence for any statement. Example
drawings are written to `out/` by

```sh
lake env lean --run scripts/render_examples.lean
```
