# string-diagrams-lean

A Lean 4 library for diagrammatic reasoning: typed diagram syntax, presentations by generators and relations, interpretations, and Lean-checked diagram rewriting.

## Contents

### Core

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `StringDiagrams.Signature` | `Signature`: regions (0-cells), strand colours (1-cells), generators (2-cells) with typed boundaries and a parity; objects `Obj` and layers `Layer` (`id ⊗ g ⊗ id`) with well-formedness. Monoidal signatures are the case of a single region. |
| `StringDiagrams.Syntax` | The free 2-category: morphisms are well-typed lists of layers (`Diagram`), composed by concatenation; whiskering and retyping along equalities of objects (which never changes the underlying layers, so widths may be symbolic). |
| `StringDiagrams.Presentation` | `Presentation S R`: relations between `R`-linear combinations of diagrams. `Presentation.Presented` is the `R`-linear category obtained by quotienting by the tensor ideal generated by the relations and the (Koszul-signed) interchange law. `lin_rel_cast`, `diag_interchange`: relations and interchange at arbitrary whiskered positions. |
| `StringDiagrams.Interpretation` | Functors determined by images of layers; `Presentation.lift` (soundness): a functor killing all whiskered relations and interchange instances descends to an `R`-linear functor on the presented category. |
| `StringDiagrams.Whisker` | Whiskering `u ⊗ f ⊗ v` on the presented category (`Presentation.whisk`), linear and functorial. |
| `StringDiagrams.Generation` | Induction principles: morphisms are linear combinations of diagrams, diagrams are composites of layers (`Presentation.hom_induction_layers`). |

### Example: odd dots

`StringDiagrams.Examples.Exterior`: a single odd dot with `dot ≫ dot = 0`; distinct dots anticommute by the super interchange law alone (`x_mul_x_anticomm`).

### Example: nilHecke algebras

Source: M. Khovanov, A. D. Lauda, *A diagrammatic approach to categorification of quantum groups I*, [arXiv:0803.4121v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.4121v2), §2.2, Example 3 (p. 10). Strands and variables are numbered from `0` here (the paper's `x_a`, `∂_a` are `x n (a-1)`, `ψ n (a-1)`).

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `StringDiagrams.Examples.NilHecke.DividedDifference` | Divided difference operators `divDiff i` on `MvPolynomial ℕ R` over any commutative ring, defined without division; all nilHecke relations for `divDiff` and multiplication by variables (`divDiff_divDiff`, `divDiff_braid`, `mulX_divDiff_sub`, `divDiff_mulX_sub`, `divDiff_comm`, `mulX_divDiff_comm`, `mulX_comm`). |
| `StringDiagrams.Examples.NilHecke.Basic` | The nilHecke presentation `NilHecke.pres R` (dot, crossing; relations (2.17)–(2.19) and the dot slides) and the dots `x R n i`, crossings `ψ R n i` in `End` of `n` strands. |
| `StringDiagrams.Examples.NilHecke.Relations` | The nilHecke relations for every width `n` and position `i`: `ψ_mul_ψ`, `ψ_braid`, `x_mul_ψ_sub_ψ_mul_x`, `ψ_mul_x_sub_x_mul_ψ`, `x_mul_x_comm`, `x_mul_ψ_comm`, `ψ_mul_ψ_comm`. |

All results are free of `sorry`; the headline declarations depend only on the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

## Building

Requires [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan). Toolchain
`leanprover/lean4:v4.19.0` and Mathlib `c44e0c8ee63ca166450922a373c7409c5d26b00b` are
pinned.

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build
```
